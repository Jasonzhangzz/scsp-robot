\subsection{Proof of Closest Approximation}\label{proof:closest_approximation}
Let $\boldsymbol{W}_{ii} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ be the original Delassus matrix. We seek a diagonal matrix $D = \operatorname{diag}(d_1, \dots, d_n)$ 
that minimizes the Frobenius norm of the approximation error:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{D}^* = \arg \min_{\substack{\boldsymbol{D}}} 
    \left\| \boldsymbol{W}_{ii} - \boldsymbol{D} \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^2.
\end{equation}
Expanding the Frobenius norm, we have
\begin{equation}
    \| \boldsymbol{W}_{ii} - \boldsymbol{D} \|_\text{F}^2 = \sum_{j \neq k} |(\boldsymbol{W}_{ii})_{jk}|^2 + \sum_{j=1}^{n} |(\boldsymbol{W}_{ii})_{jj} - d_j|^2.
\end{equation}
The off-diagonal terms are not affected by choosing $D$, and so the minimization reduces to independent scalar problems for each diagonal entry:
\begin{equation}
d_j^* = \arg \min_{d_j} |(\boldsymbol{W}_{ii})_{jj} - d_j|^2, \quad j = 1, \dots, n.
\end{equation}
It is immediate that the minimizer is $d_j^* = (\boldsymbol{W}_{ii})_{jj}$. 
Hence \eqref{equ:max_decomposition} is the closest diagonal approximation in the Frobenius norm with a small regularization term for numerical stability.

\subsection{Local Motion-Prediction Accuracy of SCM}
\label{app:scm_motion_accuracy}

We compare (29) and (31) using identical current states,
robot contact inputs, contact geometry, and model parameters.
Let $\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{env}}$ and
$\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{env}}$ denote the nonnegative
multipliers in (21) and their SCM predictions, respectively.
Let $D\succ0$ collect the diagonal approximations in (27),
$E:=W_{\mathrm{env}}-D$, and $M_o\succ0$, $R\succ0$.
We use $\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_A
=(\boldsymbol{z}^\top A\boldsymbol{z})^{1/2}$ and define the
geometry-dependent motion gain
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma_{\mathrm{env}}
    :=\|M_o^{-1/2}\tilde J_{\mathrm{env}}^\top D^{-1/2}\|_2.
    \label{eq:appB_geometry_gain}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Directional accuracy}

In the zero-gap setting of (28), reference complementarity
and (29)--(31) give
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    &h^2(\boldsymbol{v}^+)^\top M_o
      (\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}^+-\boldsymbol{v}^+)\\
    &\quad=(\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{env}}
             -\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{env}})^\top
             h\tilde J_{\mathrm{env}}\boldsymbol{v}^+\\
    &\quad\geq(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{env}}
             -\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{env}})^\top
             R\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{env}}\\
    &\quad\geq-\frac14
             \hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{env}}^\top
             R\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{env}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:appB_direction_proof}
\end{equation}
where the inequalities follow from nonnegative multipliers
and slack, followed by completing the square. For nonzero
velocity changes, their inertia-weighted angle satisfies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \cos\theta_{M_o}
    &:=\frac{(\boldsymbol{v}^+)^\top M_o\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}^+}
    {\|\boldsymbol{v}^+\|_{M_o}
     \|\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}^+\|_{M_o}}\\
    &\geq\frac{\|\boldsymbol{v}^+\|_{M_o}}
    {\|\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}^+\|_{M_o}}
    -\frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{env}}^\top
    R\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{env}}}
    {4h^2\|\boldsymbol{v}^+\|_{M_o}
     \|\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}^+\|_{M_o}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:appB_direction_consistency}
\end{equation}

For the stationary-environment tasks, the force limits in (32)
constrain robot-induced loading. With bounded non-contact loads,
fixed contact geometry, and retained diagonal responses bounded
away from zero, (26) also bounds the predicted environment
reactions. Small $R$ then limits the quadratic regularization
contribution. Its normalized contribution in
\eqref{eq:appB_direction_consistency} is small during
motion-producing steps for which the velocity changes are not
negligible relative to the contact loading.

When the normalized correction is negligible, a large angular error requires substantial overestimation of the motion magnitude. Conversely, closely matched magnitudes imply small angular errors. The weak-coupling analysis below supports such magnitude agreement away from near-static force balance

Most tasks considered here emphasize pose regulation rather
than highly dynamic motion. Accordingly, the stable-pose
objective in (34) uses the predicted motion to identify contacts
that promote goal-directed pose changes, rather than to track
an exact velocity. Preserving these motion trends is therefore
particularly important for CSO contact selection.

\subsubsection{Magnitude accuracy}

Since $W_{\mathrm{env}}=D+E$, the reference complementarity
conditions and SCM prediction can be written as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{env}}
    &=\max\!\left[-D^{-1}
      (\boldsymbol{g}_{\mathrm{env}}
      +E\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{env}}),0\right],\\
    \hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{env}}
    &=\max(-D^{-1}\boldsymbol{g}_{\mathrm{env}},0).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:appB_force_fixed_point}
\end{equation}
The scalar map $\max(\cdot,0)$ does not amplify differences.
Applying this property componentwise and summing with weights
$D_{ii}$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \|\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{env}}
      -\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{env}}\|_D
    \leq\|D^{-1/2}E\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{env}}\|_2.
    \label{eq:appB_force_accuracy}
\end{equation}
Together with (29) and (31), this implies
\begin{equation}
    h\|\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}^+-\boldsymbol{v}^+\|_{M_o}
    \leq\Gamma_{\mathrm{env}}
    \|D^{-1/2}E\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{env}}\|_2.
    \label{eq:appB_motion_accuracy}
\end{equation}
The discarded coupling and diagonal-perturbation effects are
therefore weighted by the current contact loading. Small
$\|D^{-1/2}E\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{env}}\|_2$
yields small force errors and, with a moderate
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{env}}$, small motion errors. This characterizes
the weak-coupling regime targeted by Assumption~2 and (25).

Contact orientations, moment arms, and object inertia determine
$E$, $D$, and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{env}}$, affecting both coupling
strength and the conversion of force errors into motion errors.
Robot-force magnitude affects
$\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{env}}$ through
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{b}}_o$ in (19), with the robot-induced load
limited by (32). Prediction accuracy therefore depends jointly
on contact geometry and current loading, including gravity,
rather than on coupling strength alone.

\subsubsection{Contact transitions}

A newly established object--environment contact introduces a
reaction absent from the SCM rollout. Its effect is not included
in $E\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathrm{env}}$ for the previous contact
set, and the fixed-set estimates do not control the full
transition error. SCSP incorporates updated object states and
contact estimates through high-frequency replanning, while the
ranking strategy in (40) temporally filters contact-switching
decisions. Together with CPO using
$\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}_r^*$ as guidance rather than a prescribed
contact, these mechanisms limit the persistence of inaccurate
priors and reduce unnecessary switching. When contact estimates
are refreshed promptly relative to object motion, they help
prevent transient SCM errors from persistently biasing contact
selection.

\subsection{Proof of Lemma 4}
\label{proof:lemma4}
Consider the lift-and-place cost 
$\ell_{\text{lp}} = (1-\gamma) \, \ell_{\text{lift}} + \gamma\, \ell_{\text{place}}$. 
We analyze the different cases of the switching parameter $\gamma$.

\textbf{Case 1:} When $\gamma = 1$,
$\ell_{\text{lp}} = \ell_{\text{place}}$. 
The placing objective only guides the robot to reference points 
$\boldsymbol{p}_{\text{ref}} \in \{\boldsymbol{\hat{p}}^*_{\text{r}}, \boldsymbol{p}_{\text{near}}\}$ 
on the object, it does not obstruct the first-order improvement of the objective since $\boldsymbol{\hat{p}}^*_{\text{r}}$ and $\boldsymbol{p}_{\text{near}}$ is the optimal or an acceptable suboptimal solution returned by CSO for improving $\ell_{\text{obj}}$.

\textbf{Case 2:} When $\gamma = 0$, $\ell_{\text{lp}} = \ell_{\text{lift}}$ and the lifting phase moves the end-effector away from the object to an intermediate pose 
above the reference contact. Since CPO uses a short horizon, it is usually unable to reach $\mathcal{S}_{\text{contact}}$ while moving away from the object, and thus unable to obtain the gradient of $\ell_{\text{obj}}$. Therefore, $\ell_{\text{obj}}$ does not affect $\ell_{\text{lp}}$.

Since $\ell_{\text{lp}}$ is a convex combination of lift and place costs, $\ell_{\text{lp}}$ and $\ell_{\text{obj}}$ are non-conflicting objectives.
