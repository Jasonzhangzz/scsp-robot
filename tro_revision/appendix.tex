



\subsection{Proof of Closest Approximation}\label{proof:closest_approximation}
Let $\boldsymbol{A}_{ii}=\boldsymbol{W}_{ii}-\boldsymbol{R}_{ii} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ be the unregularized contact mobility matrix. We seek a diagonal matrix $D = \operatorname{diag}(d_1, \dots, d_n)$ 
that minimizes the Frobenius norm of the approximation error:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{D}^* = \arg \min_{\substack{\boldsymbol{D}}} 
    \left\| \boldsymbol{A}_{ii} - \boldsymbol{D} \right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^2.
\end{equation}
Expanding the Frobenius norm, we have
\begin{equation}
    \| \boldsymbol{A}_{ii} - \boldsymbol{D} \|_\text{F}^2 = \sum_{j \neq k} |(\boldsymbol{A}_{ii})_{jk}|^2 + \sum_{j=1}^{n} |(\boldsymbol{A}_{ii})_{jj} - d_j|^2.
\end{equation}
The off-diagonal terms are not affected by choosing $D$, and so the minimization reduces to independent scalar problems for each diagonal entry:
\begin{equation}
d_j^* = \arg \min_{d_j} |(\boldsymbol{A}_{ii})_{jj} - d_j|^2, \quad j = 1, \dots, n.
\end{equation}
It is immediate that the minimizer is $d_j^* = (\boldsymbol{A}_{ii})_{jj}$. 
Hence $\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{A}_{ii})$ is the closest diagonal approximation in the Frobenius norm. Equation~\eqref{equ:max_decomposition} adds $\epsilon\boldsymbol{I}=\boldsymbol{R}_{ii}$, so that $\boldsymbol{D}=\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{W}_{ii})$ carries the same regularization as the coupled matrix $\boldsymbol{W}_{\mathrm{env}}$.
